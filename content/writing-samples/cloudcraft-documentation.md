## Background

When Datadog acquired Cloudcraft, a tool for visualizing cloud architecture, there was a need to introduce the product to Datadog customers while continuing to support existing Cloudcraft users.

I took ownership of the Cloudcraft documentation, which gave me the opportunity to build out an entirely new product documentation section within the Datadog documentation site.

Part of the challenge was determining which content could be carried over, what needed to be rewritten to align with Datadog's documentation standards, and where new guidance was needed as Cloudcraft became more integrated with the Datadog platform. Throughout this process, I also continued to maintain the standalone documentation for existing Cloudcraft customers.

## My Role

One of the things I enjoyed most about this project was learning the product from the ground up. I spent time exploring Cloudcraft, testing its capabilities, and understanding how customers use it to visualize and manage their cloud environments.

My responsibilities included:

- Designing the information architecture for the new documentation section.
- Authoring documentation from the ground up.
- Testing product functionality and customer workflows.
- Working with Product and Engineering to validate technical accuracy.
- Documenting integrations with other Datadog products.
- Maintaining the existing standalone Cloudcraft documentation.

## The Challenge

Cloudcraft was particularly interesting to document because it wasn't an isolated product. Its capabilities extended across cloud providers and several Datadog products, including APM, Infrastructure Monitoring, Cloud Security, and Cloud Cost Management.

I needed to consider how customers might discover Cloudcraft through these different products and ensure they could find the guidance they needed, regardless of where they started.

## The Outcome

The project established a dedicated home for Cloudcraft within the Datadog documentation site, giving customers a place to learn about the product, get started, and explore its capabilities.

It remains one of my favorite documentation projects because I had the opportunity to own the process from the initial research and information architecture through writing, testing, and publication.
